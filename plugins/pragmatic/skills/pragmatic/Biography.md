## Andy Hunt — Solo Biography

- **(undated, secondary school)** — born and raised in the United States. Wikipedia article does not list a confirmed birth year (flagged for citations).
- **(early career)** — Started in the CP/M and S100-bus / Heathkit era. First real program — *"a combination text editor and database manager"* — written for an Ohio Scientific Challenger 4P. Coded in 6502 assembler, modified operating systems. Source: toolshed.com/about.html.
- **1981** — Wrote his first **commercial program**, a Manufacturing Resources Planning system. Source: toolshed.com.
- **(mid-career)** — Self-taught Unix and C. Worked across BSD and System V. Moved into electronic pre-press and computer graphics on Silicon Graphics machines. Source: toolshed.com.
- **early 1990s** — Began consulting. Source: toolshed.com.
- **October 1999** — Co-authored *The Pragmatic Programmer* with Dave Thomas (Addison-Wesley). Source: Wikipedia.
- **2000/2001** — Co-authored *Programming Ruby* (the Pickaxe) with Dave Thomas — first English-language book on Ruby. Source: Wikipedia.
- **11–13 February 2001** — One of the **17 original signers** of the Manifesto for Agile Software Development at Snowbird, Utah. Source: agilemanifesto.org.
- **2003** — Co-founded **The Pragmatic Bookshelf** publishing imprint with Dave Thomas. Source: pragprog.com/about/.
- **2006** — Co-authored *Practices of an Agile Developer* with Venkat Subramaniam (Pragmatic Bookshelf). Source: Pragmatic Bookshelf.
- **2008** — Solo-authored ***Pragmatic Thinking and Learning: Refactor Your Wetware*** (Pragmatic Bookshelf). Dreyfus model applied to programmers; R-mode vs L-mode; Knowledge Portfolio expanded. Source: pragprog.com/titles/ahptl.
- **2017–2021** — Published fiction trilogy *Conglommora*, *Conglommora Found*, *Weatherly Hall*. Source: Wikipedia.
- **September 2019** — Co-authored *The Pragmatic Programmer, 20th Anniversary Edition* with Dave Thomas. ISBN 978-0135957059. Source: pragprog.com/titles/tpp20.
- **2023** — Retired from running The Pragmatic Bookshelf; Dave Thomas continues. Source: pragprog.com/about/.
- **(ongoing)** — Active musician (trumpet, flugel horn, keyboards) under "Strange & Special Air Productions"; woodworker. Source: Wikipedia / toolshed.com.
- **Location:** Raleigh, North Carolina, USA. Source: linkedin.com/in/pragmaticandy.

## Pragmatic Bookshelf — Joint History

- **2003** — **Founded** by Andy Hunt and Dave Thomas. Source: pragprog.com/about/.
- **Origin story** — They co-authored *The Pragmatic Programmer* (1999) and *Programming Ruby* (2000) under Addison-Wesley. Frustrated by the speed and rigidity of traditional tech publishers — and especially by how slow the publishing pipeline was relative to fast-moving tech topics like Ruby — they decided to *"create an agile publishing business."* Source: toolshed.com.
- **Mission (verbatim):** *"to improve the lives of software developers."* Source: pragprog.com/about/.
- **Innovations they pioneered:**
  - **Beta book** model — readers buy the in-progress book at PDF beta and receive updates as the manuscript is revised. *"we pioneered the 'beta book' concept"* — pragprog.com/about/.
  - **DRM-free** since day one. *"Our titles do not contain any Digital Restrictions Management, and have always been DRM-free."* — pragprog.com/about/.
  - Markdown / single-source-multiple-format toolchain — the *PragProg* publishing pipeline (XML-based; produces print, PDF, ePub, Kindle from one source).
- **Notable titles published:** *Programming Ruby* (2nd ed onward, from 2004), *Programming Elixir* (2014), *Pragmatic Thinking and Learning* (2008), *Practices of an Agile Developer* (2006), *Agile Web Development with Rails* (DHH), *Seven Languages in Seven Weeks* (Bruce Tate, 2010), *Release It!* (Michael Nygard).
- **Royalty model:** Higher author royalty than traditional publishers; no advance in the conventional sense.

## *The Pragmatic Programmer* — Book History

- **1st edition:** **October 1999**, Addison-Wesley. ISBN 0-201-61622-X. Subtitle: *From Journeyman to Master*. **70 numbered tips.** Source: Wikipedia: The Pragmatic Programmer.
- **20th Anniversary Edition:** **September 2019**, Addison-Wesley. ISBN 978-0135957059. New subtitle: *Your Journey to Mastery*. **100 numbered tips** — 30 added, several rewritten, some retired. Source: pragprog.com/titles/tpp20.
- **Concepts the book popularized:** DRY, rubber duck debugging, broken windows theory as applied to code, stone soup parable, orthogonality, tracer bullets, prototype vs spike, good-enough software, knowledge portfolio. Source: Wikipedia.
- **Reception:**
  - *"regularly featured on 'Top Ten' lists"* of programming books.
  - *"many corporations issue it to their new hires"*.
  - Used as a textbook in university software-engineering courses.
  - Repeatedly selected to "best programming book of all time" community polls.
- **Translations:** Multiple — confirmed Spanish, Portuguese, German, French, Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Russian, Polish among others.
- **Awards (UNVERIFIED):** Some web summaries assert the 1999 book won the Jolt Productivity Award; others credit the *Pragmatic Starter Kit* (2003). Treat as **unverified within the research budget** — confirm before citing.

## *Programming Ruby* — The Pickaxe Book

- **1st edition:** Authors **Dave Thomas and Andy Hunt**. Published by **Addison-Wesley**, 2000/2001 (manuscript freely available under Open Publication License from 2000; complete first edition publication 2001 per Wikipedia). Source: Wikipedia: Programming Ruby.
- **Significance:** The **first English-language book on Ruby**. Single-handedly introduced Ruby to the Western developer audience.
- **Pickaxe nickname:** *"commonly known as 'The PickAxe' because of the pickaxe on the cover."* — Wikipedia, verbatim.
- **Chad Fowler's role:** Joined as **co-author from the 2nd edition (2004) onward** — *not* on the 1st edition. (Community lore sometimes credits him on the 1st ed; Wikipedia attributes the 1st ed to Thomas + Hunt only. **Flag for skill-builder verification.**)
- **Foreword by Matz:** Frequently asserted in community lore but not located in a primary source within research budget. **Flag for verification.**
- **All editions:** 1st 2001 (Addison-Wesley); 2nd 2004 (PragProg, Ruby 1.8); 3rd 2010 (Ruby 1.9); 4th 2013 (Ruby 1.9 & 2.0); 5th 2023 (Ruby 3.3, updated by Noel Rappin).

## *Pragmatic Thinking and Learning: Refactor Your Wetware* (Andy Hunt 2008)

- **Publisher / Year:** Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2008.
- **ISBN:** 978-1934356050.
- **Author:** Andy Hunt (solo).
- **Subtitle:** "Refactor Your Wetware".

**Verbatim from publisher page:**
> *"Software development happens in your head. Not in an editor, IDE, or design tool."*
> *"You'll learn how our brains are wired, and how to take advantage of your brain's architecture."*
> *"You'll learn new tricks and tips to learn more, faster, and retain more of what you learn."*

Source: pragprog.com/titles/ahptl/

**Key concepts:**
- **Dreyfus model of skill acquisition:** five stages — Novice → Advanced Beginner → Competent → Proficient → Expert. Each stage has distinctive needs. Implication: training, hiring, mentoring should differ by stage.
- **R-mode vs L-mode** (adapted from Betty Edwards): L-mode = linear/verbal/analytic; R-mode = holistic/intuitive/pattern-matching. The expert integrates BOTH, not just maximizes L-mode.
- **Knowledge Portfolio** expanded — treat learning as a managed investment portfolio: diversify, invest regularly, review periodically, accept calculated risks.

## *Practices of an Agile Developer* (Andy + Venkat 2006)

- **Publisher / Year:** Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2006.
- **Authors:** Andy Hunt and **Venkat Subramaniam**.
- **Format:** A field-guide-style book of agile practices, each presented as a paired anecdotal pattern: *"Devil sits on your shoulder and whispers a bad practice"* / *"Angel sits on your shoulder and whispers the agile practice instead."*
- **Influence:** Tone influenced subsequent agile practice books.
