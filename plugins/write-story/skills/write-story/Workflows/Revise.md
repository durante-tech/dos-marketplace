# Revise -- Multi-Pass Revision

Multi-pass revision for consistency, voice, anti-cliche enforcement, and prose polish.

## When to Use

User says "revise", "polish", "edit", "this chapter feels flat", "improve the writing". Applied to drafted chapters.

## Workflow

### Pass 1: Story Bible Consistency

Check the chapter against the story bible:
- Does each scene advance the correct milestones?
- Are there contradictions with previous chapters?
- Is the sacred flaw progression on track?
- Are mystery plants/reveals happening at the right time?
- Do relationship dynamics match the planned evolution?

Flag any deviations. Fix or update the bible if the deviation is an improvement.

### Pass 2: Character Voice Consistency

For each character in the chapter:
- Does their dialogue sound like them (not like any other character)?
- Are their actions consistent with their established personality?
- Is their internal voice (if POV character) distinct?
- Do they use their characteristic speech patterns, vocabulary, and rhythms?

Test: cover the dialogue tags. Can you tell who's speaking from the words alone?

### Pass 3: Anti-Cliche System

Search and replace:
- **Dead metaphors:** "heart sank", "blood ran cold", "time stood still" -> find fresh alternatives
- **Generic descriptions:** "beautiful sunset", "piercing blue eyes", "dark and stormy night" -> make specific
- **Stated emotions:** "she felt angry", "he was sad" -> show through action and sensation
- **Adverb crutches:** "said angrily", "walked quickly" -> stronger verbs
- **Filler phrases:** "seemed to", "began to", "started to" -> direct action

### Pass 4: Prose Polish with Rhetorical Figures

Identify the 3-5 most important moments in the chapter. For each, check:
- Is there a rhetorical figure deployed for emphasis?
- Does the sentence rhythm match the emotional beat?
- Is the word order optimal for impact (most important word at the end)?

Apply Mark Forsyth's figures where they'll land hardest:
- Moments of revelation -> chiasmus or antithesis
- Building tension -> anadiplosis or climax (ascending tricolon)
- Emotional peaks -> epistrophe or anaphora
- Opening lines -> hyperbaton or prolepsis

### Pass 5: Final Read-Through

Read the chapter as a reader would. Check:
- Does the opening hook create immediate curiosity?
- Is there a reason to keep reading at every page?
- Does the ending create momentum?
- Does it feel fresh, or does it feel like "AI writing"?

## Validation

- [ ] Story bible consistency verified
- [ ] Character voices are distinct
- [ ] Zero dead metaphors or cliche phrases
- [ ] Rhetorical figures deployed at key moments
- [ ] Final read-through passes the "would I keep reading?" test
